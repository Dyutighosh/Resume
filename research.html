Ongoing Current Work

Privacy-Preserving Encrypted Computations

Encrypted keyword search and abuse detection in end-to-end encrypted messaging systems

We are developing privacy-preserving methods for keyword search and coordinated-abuse detection in end-to-end encrypted messaging without giving the service access to message contents. The work combines Fully Homomorphic Encryption and Secure Two-Party Computation to evaluate searches and aggregate only sufficiently supported abuse signals over encrypted data. The aim is to reveal only matching results or threshold-supported evidence while hiding unrelated messages, search terms, and low-support reports.

Machine Learning for Grid Security

Customer-side non-technical loss detection from low-granularity billing data

We are developing an interpretable, label-free framework for detecting customer-side non-technical electricity losses from coarse monthly billing records. It combines tariff-slab and season-aware elasticity modeling with self-supervised forecasts of consumption, billed amount, and unit price. An ensemble of linear, regularized, robust, and gradient-boosting models establishes account-specific baselines, whereas residual magnitude, persistence, volatility, model agreement, and dispersion are combined into a prioritized inspection list. The framework is validated using real-world utility records and controlled loss-injection benchmarks.

Privacy-Aware Cyber-Physical Control Systems

Privacy-aware cyber-physical control under intentionally skipped executions

We are developing a privacy-aware framework for cyber-physical control systems in which temporal correlations in differentially private feedback can still allow recursive estimators to infer protected system states. The framework predicts a short operating horizon and selects a bounded schedule of controller executions, intentionally retaining the preceding control command at selected instants rather than issuing a new update. This disrupts exploitable temporal correlations while preserving control utility and closed-loop stability. The approach is evaluated through real-time closed-loop testing under representative operating conditions and multiple privacy-noise models to assess privacy, accuracy, stability, and timing feasibility.

Side Projects

Hardware-in-the-loop smart-grid testbed at IIT-KGP

Established a hardware-in-the-loop smart-grid testbed integrating an OPAL-RT real-time simulator, a National Instruments CompactRIO controller, phasor measurement units, a phasor data concentrator, a voltage/current power amplifier, a GPS clock, and an automation controller. The platform supported closed-loop, real-time experiments to analyze how cyberattacks affect power-system control and communication and to test countermeasures, including secure and privacy-preserving communication protocols, under realistic operating and timing conditions.

Secure smart meter prototype

Developed a low-cost smart meter on a Raspberry Pi 4 for integration with a smart-grid testbed. The prototype incorporated a spoofing-resistant, physically unclonable function-based authenticated key-exchange protocol for secure device communication. A CME bench-top chamber reproduced changing environmental conditions to assess SRAM-PUF reliability and evaluate the performance of the authenticated key-exchange protocol.

PLC security assessment

Constructed a closed-loop experimental setup using National Instruments CompactRIO as the programmable logic controller and OPAL-RT as the real-time plant simulator. The setup implemented representative smart-grid control functions and supported vulnerability analysis under multiple attack scenarios, including the physics-aware PLC rootkit described in the HARVEY paper at NDSS 2017. It also enabled real-time testing of countermeasures and analysis of the operational consequences of compromised controller behavior under realistic operating and timing conditions.
